## 1. Definitions

**1.1 "FERPA"** means the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act, 20 U.S.C. § 1232g, and its
implementing regulations at 34 C.F.R. Part 99.

**1.2 "Education Records"** has the meaning given in 34 C.F.R. § 99.3.

**1.3 "Student Data"** means any personally identifiable information, as defined in 34 C.F.R. § 99.3,
that is contained in Education Records and is provided to, or created by, the Provider in the course
of delivering the Service. Student Data includes the categories listed in **Schedule A** and includes
material the student authors inside the Service, such as conversation transcripts and submitted
essays.

**1.4 "De-identified Data"** means data from which all direct and indirect identifiers listed in
Schedule A have been permanently removed, and which cannot reasonably be used alone or in combination
with other reasonably available information to identify a student, consistent with 34 C.F.R. § 99.31(b).

**1.5 "Subprocessor"** means a third party engaged by the Provider that processes Student Data on the
Provider's behalf. The Subprocessors approved as of the Effective Date are listed in **Schedule B**.

**1.6 "Security Incident"** means any confirmed unauthorised access to, acquisition of, disclosure of,
alteration of, or destruction of Student Data in the Provider's or a Subprocessor's custody.

**1.7 "Authorised Purpose"** means the purpose set out in Section 3.1 and no other purpose.

## 2. Roles of the Parties

**2.1 The School holds the records.** The School is, and remains at all times, the owner of and the
entity in control of all Student Data. Nothing in this DPA or in the Principal Agreement transfers
ownership of Student Data to the Provider. The Provider holds Student Data only as a custodian acting
on the School's instructions.

**2.2 School official designation.** The School designates the Provider as a "school official" with a
"legitimate educational interest" in Student Data under 34 C.F.R. § 99.31(a)(1)(i)(B). The Provider
accordingly:

  (a) performs an institutional service or function for which the School would otherwise use its own
      employees;

  (b) is under the direct control of the School with respect to the use and maintenance of Education
      Records, and will act on the School's documented instructions in respect of Student Data;

  (c) uses Student Data only for the Authorised Purpose; and

  (d) will not redisclose Student Data to any party except as expressly permitted by Section 4 or as
      directed in writing by the School.

**2.3 The School's obligations.** The School is responsible for (i) issuing the notices and obtaining
any consents required under FERPA, the Protection of Pupil Rights Amendment, and applicable state law
before students use the Service; (ii) including "school officials" and the criteria for "legitimate
educational interest" in its annual FERPA notification, in a form that covers contracted providers;
and (iii) the accuracy of the roster data it supplies.

**2.4 Children under 13.** Where the Service is made available to students under the age of 13, the
School provides consent under the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 6501–6506, on
behalf of the parents of those students, in its capacity as their educational institution, and
warrants that it has authority to do so.

## 3. Permitted and prohibited processing

**3.1 Authorised Purpose.** The Provider may process Student Data solely to: operate, maintain, and
support the Service for the School; generate the analyses and reports the Service exists to produce;
provide technical support to the School's users; secure the Service and investigate Security
Incidents; carry out the service refinement described in Section 3.2; and comply with law.

**3.2 Service refinement.** The Provider uses conversation transcripts and submitted work to check and
improve how accurately the Service reads them. This is ordinary maintenance of the Service the School
has engaged, is carried out by the Provider's own personnel, and produces changes to the Service
rather than findings about any student. The Provider does not use Student Data to train AI models,
does not sell it, and does not publish anything about an individual student. Any activity designed to
produce generalisable knowledge — including published validity evidence for the Service's measures —
falls outside this Section and requires a separate written agreement under Section 3.6.

**3.3 Prohibited uses.** The Provider will not, and will contractually prohibit each Subprocessor
from:

  (a) selling, renting, trading, or otherwise making Student Data available for consideration;

  (b) using Student Data for targeted, behavioural, or contextual advertising, or disclosing it to any
      party for that purpose;

  (c) building a commercial profile of any student for any purpose other than the Authorised Purpose;

  (d) using Student Data to train, fine-tune, ground, or otherwise improve any machine-learning model,
      including any model of the Provider, of a Subprocessor, or of any third party;

  (e) using Student Data in marketing or promotional material, or disclosing it to a prospective
      customer;

  (f) conducting research intended to produce generalisable knowledge, except under Section 3.6; or

  (g) retaining Student Data beyond the periods set out in Section 7.

**3.4 AI processing.** The Service transmits conversation transcripts, submitted essays, and
assignment text to a hosted large language model operated by the Subprocessor identified in Schedule B
for the sole purpose of generating the student's conversation and the resulting analysis. The Provider
warrants that its contract with that Subprocessor prohibits the Subprocessor from using that content
to train or improve the Subprocessor's models, and from retaining it other than transiently for abuse
monitoring for no longer than `[RETENTION PERIOD STATED IN SUBPROCESSOR TERMS]`. The Provider will not
enable any feature of that Subprocessor's service that permits such training or extended retention.

**3.5 De-identified Data.** The Provider may create and retain De-identified Data, and may use it to
maintain and improve the Service and to publish aggregate research, provided that the Provider (i)
does not attempt to re-identify it and does not permit any third party to do so; (ii) does not release
it in any form that permits identification of an individual student, teacher, School, or class; and
(iii) publishes no aggregate figure derived from a group smaller than `[MINIMUM CELL SIZE, e.g. 10]`
students. De-identified Data is not Student Data and survives the deletion required by Section 7.

**3.6 Research.** Any activity designed to develop or contribute to generalisable knowledge — as
distinct from the service refinement permitted by Section 3.2 — requires a separate written agreement
between the Parties before any Student Data is used for it. That agreement will specify the purpose,
scope, and duration of the work, the data to be used, and the date by which personally identifiable
information will be destroyed, consistent with 34 C.F.R. § 99.31(a)(6). Where the School is a
postsecondary institution, the Provider will additionally obtain the institution's ethical review
determination and, where that determination requires it, the separate and revocable consent of each
participating eligible student.

**3.7 Change of purpose.** The Provider will not materially change how Student Data is processed, nor
add a processing purpose, without the School's prior written consent.

## 4. Disclosure

**4.1 Permitted recipients.** The Provider may disclose Student Data only to: (i) the School and users
the School has authorised; (ii) Subprocessors under Section 5; (iii) the Provider's own personnel who
need access for the Authorised Purpose and are bound by confidentiality obligations at least as
protective as this DPA; and (iv) as required by law under Section 4.2.

**4.2 Compelled disclosure.** If the Provider receives a subpoena, court order, or other legal demand
for Student Data, it will, unless legally prohibited, notify the School before disclosing, provide a
copy of the demand, and give the School a reasonable opportunity to seek a protective order. The
Provider will not disclose more than the demand compels.

**4.3 Requests from parents, students, and third parties.** The Provider will not respond
substantively to any request from a parent, eligible student, or other third party for access to,
correction of, or deletion of Student Data. It will redirect the requester to the School and notify
the School within `[5]` business days.

## 6. Security

**6.1 Standard.** The Provider will implement and maintain administrative, physical, and technical
safeguards appropriate to the sensitivity of Student Data and no less protective than the measures in
**Schedule C**, which it will not materially degrade during the term.

**6.2 Access control.** Access to Student Data by Provider personnel is limited to the minimum
necessary for the Authorised Purpose, is individually authenticated, and is revoked within `[24]`
hours of a person ceasing to require it.

**6.3 Security Incident notification.** The Provider will notify the School of a Security Incident
affecting the School's Student Data without undue delay and in any event **within `[72]` hours** of
confirming it. The notification will describe, to the extent known: the nature of the incident, the
categories and approximate number of students affected, the data involved, the likely consequences,
the remediation taken and planned, and a contact point. The Provider will supplement the notification
as facts develop, cooperate with the School's investigation, and not notify affected individuals or
regulators about the School's students without the School's prior written approval unless independently
required by law.

**6.4 Incident costs.** Where a Security Incident arises from the Provider's or a Subprocessor's breach
of this DPA, the Provider will bear the reasonable costs of investigation, of notifying affected
individuals and regulators, and of `[credit monitoring / identity protection services for [12] months]`
where required by law or reasonably necessary.

**6.5 Assessments.** On no more than `[one]` occasion per year, and on `[30]` days' notice, the
Provider will complete a written security questionnaire from the School and provide any then-current
third-party audit report or penetration test summary it holds. `[Optional: an on-site or remote audit
right, at the School's expense, on reasonable notice.]`

## 7. Retention, return, and deletion

**7.1 Retention limit.** The Provider will retain Student Data only for as long as it is needed for
the Authorised Purpose, and in no event longer than the periods in Section 7.2.

**7.2 Deletion triggers.** The Provider will delete Student Data in accordance with **Schedule D** on
the earliest to occur of:

  (a) **End of purpose.** The expiry of the retention period stated in Schedule D for that category of
      Student Data, measured from the end of the academic term or course in which the data was
      created;

  (b) **School instruction.** Receipt of a written deletion instruction from the School, whether for
      an individual student, a class, or the whole account — to be completed within `[30]` days of the
      instruction;

  (c) **Account closure.** The date a student's or teacher's account is closed or disabled by the
      School, subject to the grace period in Section 7.3; or

  (d) **Termination.** Expiry or termination of the Principal Agreement for any reason, as set out in
      Section 7.4.

**7.3 Grace period.** Following a trigger under Section 7.2(a) or 7.2(c), the Provider will hold the
affected Student Data for `[30]` days before deleting it, so that the School may retrieve it or
reverse an accidental closure. The School may waive the grace period in writing and require immediate
deletion.

**7.4 On termination.** Within `[30]` days of expiry or termination, the Provider will, at the
School's election, (i) return all Student Data to the School in the export format described in Section
7.7, or (ii) delete it; and in either case will delete all remaining copies within `[60]` days of
termination.

**7.5 Method of deletion.** Deletion means rendering Student Data permanently irretrievable by the
Provider — deleting the underlying records from live systems rather than marking them inactive,
hidden, archived, or soft-deleted. Where the Provider cannot itself erase a copy held in a
Subprocessor's system (for example, an encrypted backup on a fixed rotation), it will instead ensure
that the copy is isolated from all processing, remains subject to every obligation in this DPA, and is
destroyed on the ordinary expiry of that rotation, which will not exceed **`[35]` days**.

**7.6 Certificate of destruction.** Within `[15]` days of completing deletion under Section 7.2(b) or
7.4, the Provider will supply the School with a written certificate, signed by an officer, stating what
was deleted, the date of deletion, and the date by which any residual backup copies will expire.

**7.7 Export.** For as long as the Principal Agreement is in force and during any grace period, the
School may export its Student Data — including rosters, transcripts, submissions, and generated
analyses — in a machine-readable format `[JSON / CSV]` at no charge.

**7.8 Legal hold.** The Provider may retain Student Data past a deletion trigger only to the extent
required by law, and only for as long as required. It will notify the School of any such retention,
identify the requirement, isolate the retained data from all other processing, and delete it as soon as
the requirement lapses.

**7.9 Survival.** Sections 2.2, 3.3, 4, 6, 7, 8, and 10 survive termination in respect of any Student
Data still held.

## 8. Parent and eligible-student rights

**8.1 Access and correction.** The Provider will assist the School, at no additional charge, in
responding to a parent's or eligible student's request to inspect and review Education Records under
34 C.F.R. § 99.10, or to seek correction of records believed inaccurate or misleading under 34 C.F.R.
§ 99.20. The Provider will supply the School with the requested records, or with the means to retrieve
them, within `[10]` business days of the School's request, and will apply any correction the School
directs.

**8.2 Analyses are records too.** The Parties acknowledge that the reports and analyses the Service
generates about a student, together with the conversation transcripts they are derived from, form part
of that student's Education Records and are subject to Section 8.1.

**8.3 No independent relationship.** The Provider will not use Student Data to contact, market to, or
create an account relationship with any student or parent outside the Service.

## Schedule C — Security measures

1. **Encryption.** All data encrypted in transit over TLS 1.2 or higher. All data encrypted at rest by
   the storage platform.
2. **No direct client access to storage.** The datastore is closed to client applications by a
   deny-all security rule; every read and write is mediated by the server, which applies per-role
   authorisation on each request.
3. **Authorisation model.** A teacher can reach only the classes, assignments, students, and
   submissions within their own scope; a student can reach only their own work.
4. **Credentials.** Passwords are stored as scrypt hashes with a per-user random salt and a work
   factor above the platform default. No administrator can view a user's password. Account setup and
   password reset use single-use, expiring tokens delivered by email.
5. **Sessions.** Session cookies are HttpOnly, SameSite-restricted, and Secure in production, with a
   fixed maximum age and server-side revocation.
6. **Rate limiting.** Repeated failed logins are counted per account and per source address and
   throttled.
7. **Environment separation.** Production data for real schools is held in a dedicated cloud project,
   separate from the demonstration environment. Demonstration and seed data cannot be written to the
   production project. Production data is not copied into development or demonstration environments.
8. **Least privilege.** Service accounts and personnel hold the minimum permissions required.
9. **Logging.** Administrative actions and authentication events are logged with actor and timestamp.

## Drafting notes — resolve before this goes to a school

1. **"Deleted per FERPA" needed a defined trigger.** FERPA does not itself impose a general
   delete-after-use duty. The school-official exception the Service relies on requires direct School
   control, purpose limitation, and no redisclosure; the express destruction duty sits in the studies
   exception at 34 C.F.R. § 99.31(a)(6)(ii)(B) and, more practically, in state student-privacy statutes
   and in contract. So the deletion obligation here is written as a contractual promise with a named
   trigger and a certificate (Section 7), not as a recital of FERPA. **A blanket "all data deleted
   after use" would also delete the product's own premise** — cross-draft and cross-assignment trend
   readings need last term's data to exist. Schedule D therefore sets per-category periods. Pick them
   deliberately: if you intend to promise same-term deletion, the trend features have to be scoped to
   within a term.

2. **Data location is not yet US-only in fact.** Firestore is provisioned in `us-central1`, but the
   Vertex AI target defaults to the `global` endpoint (`app/server/school.js:72`), which Google may
   route to regions outside the United States. Either pin the Vertex location to a US region before
   signing anything containing Section 10.2, or narrow that section to storage and disclose model-call
   routing honestly.

3. **Some promises are aspirational.** Section 6.5 (audit reports), Schedule C item 10 (backups,
   penetration testing, incident response plan), and Section 10.3 (cyber insurance) describe things
   that do not exist yet. Either stand them up or strike them — a school's counsel will ask for
   evidence.

4. **Section 7.5 needs a real backup answer.** Firestore point-in-time recovery and any scheduled
   exports have their own retention windows; the `[35]` days in Section 7.5 and Schedule D must match
   what is actually configured, and there is currently no configured backup policy to match it to.
