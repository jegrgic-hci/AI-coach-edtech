## 1. What Tau Thinking does

Tau Thinking is a writing tool. You work through a task with an AI assistant, submit a draft, and the
tool produces a reading of *how you worked* — the moves you made in the conversation, how your ideas
developed, where your writing came from. Your instructor can see that reading.

It is not a grading tool and it does not judge whether your work is correct. It describes what you
did.

## 2. Your account

You need an account, provided through your institution. Keep your password to yourself, and tell us at
`[SUPPORT EMAIL]` if you think someone else has used your account.

Use the tool for your coursework. Don't try to break it, get into other people's work, scrape it, or
resell access to it.

## 3. Your work stays yours

**You own everything you write** — your conversations, your drafts, your final submissions. Nothing
here transfers copyright to us.

To run the tool at all, we need your permission to hold and process what you write. So you give us a
limited, non-exclusive licence to store, process, and analyse your submissions and conversations, for
the purposes described in Section 4 and no others. That licence ends when your content is deleted
under Section 6.

We will never publish your work, quote it publicly, or use it in marketing.

## 4. How we use your work

**We use your conversations and submitted work to check and improve how accurately the tool reads
them.** When the tool misreads something — calls a moment one thing when it was plainly another — that
is how we find it and fix it.

Alongside that, we collect ordinary usage information: which features you used, when, how long a
session ran, and technical logs.

**What we do not do:**

- We do not use your work to train AI models — not ours, not anyone else's.
- We do not sell your work or your information, or share it with advertisers.
- We do not publish anything about you as an individual.
- We do not use your work for research intended for publication. If that ever changes, we would ask
  you separately and specifically, you could say no, and saying no would not affect your grade or your
  access to the tool.

## 5. Who can see your work

- **Your instructor** — your conversations, submissions, and the readings generated from them, for the
  classes you are enrolled in.
- **Your institution**, in line with its own policies.
- **Our staff**, only where needed to run the service or help you with a problem.
- **The services we run on** — `[Google Cloud, which hosts the application and provides the AI model;
  see our privacy notice for the full list]`. They are contractually barred from using your work for
  their own purposes, including model training.
- **Anyone the law compels us to tell**, and we will tell you first unless we are legally prevented.

Nobody else.

## 7. The AI can be wrong

The AI assistant makes mistakes — it will sometimes state things confidently that are false. Check
anything that matters. The readings the tool produces are a description of your process, not a
verdict on your thinking, and they can be wrong too.

**Follow your instructor's and your institution's rules on using AI in your coursework.** This tool
being available to you does not mean any particular use of AI is permitted in any particular
assignment. That is your instructor's call.
