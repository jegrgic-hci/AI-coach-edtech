A guide for teachers

What the signals mean

The tool checks every submission for these. They are never shown to students — they surface only on your dashboard, as reasons to start a conversation.

A signal is a question, not a verdict. Every one of these has an innocent explanation as well as a worrying one, and the tool cannot tell them apart — it can only tell you that something in this submission is worth asking about. What it means is settled by talking to the student, not by reading this page.

The signals

Three things a signal is not

  • Not visible to the student. Signals appear on your surfaces only. A student's own report never mentions them, and never has.
  • Not a finding of misconduct. The tool reports what it can see in the text. Whether it amounts to anything is a judgement about a person you know and it doesn't.
  • Not connected to the agency level. A signal doesn't raise or lower where a session sits on the ladder, and a low level is not itself a signal. Two different questions — see Agency levels.

Signals are computed per submission and shown as "Worth a chat" wherever that student appears. A student can carry more than one; the dashboard collapses them into a single pill, and opening the submission shows which fired.
