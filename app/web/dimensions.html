A guide for teachers

What the four readings measure

Under the level on every report sit four readings. Each is a question asked of the transcript, the essay and your assignment together — and each comes back with evidence, not just a number.

A reading is evidence, not a number. Each of the four comes back with a sentence saying what the student did, two or three quoted moments from the chat that show it, and the one moment that cuts against it. The band from 1 to 4 is a summary of that evidence — it is the smallest part of the reading, and the part to trust least when you disagree. Read the moments.

The four questions

Did you drive the chat? Prompting Quality

What it counts

Stretches of the conversation, and who was setting the agenda in each. Not questions, and never turns — a student can ask thirty questions inside a direction the AI chose.

What it doesn't mean

It is not politeness or prompt craft. A well-worded request to be told what to think still reads low here. And a student who reframes the problem scores here — a student who argues with an answer does not; that belongs to the next question.

Where it comes from

Task initiative in dialogue analysis — Chu-Carroll & Brown, 1997. Who holds the initiative at each point in a conversation is a measure with a published coding scheme behind it, not something we invented.

Did you check what you were told? Calibrated Skepticism

What it counts

Claims the AI made that the student took a position on — corroborated, questioned, sourced, or rejected. The unit is the claim, so ten challenges to one claim is one instance, not ten.

What it doesn't mean

It does not measure whether the student was right, and it does not reward suspicion. Calibrated is the operative word: checking everything indiscriminately is not better than checking the things that carry weight.

Where it comes from

Sourcing, corroboration and contextualization — the heuristics Sam Wineburg identified in how historians read documents, and the standard scheme for measuring source evaluation.

What survived? Selective Use

What it counts

Changes the student directed to AI-supplied material, sorted into those that altered meaning and those that only altered the surface. Rewording is a change; reversing a claim is a different kind of change.

What it doesn't mean

It is not "how much did they edit." A student who rewrote every sentence and changed no argument reads low. It also only ever looks at AI-originated material — work on the student's own ideas belongs to the last question.

Where it comes from

Surface versus meaning revision — Faigley & Witte, 1981, the taxonomy that established the difference between editing a text and changing what it says.

Is the thinking yours? Original Contribution

What it counts

Ideas in the finished piece, traced back to where they first appear — the student's side of the chat, the AI's, built jointly, or nowhere in the chat at all (which usually means the student brought it with them).

What it doesn't mean

It is not originality in the sense you would grade. A commonplace idea the student genuinely arrived at reads higher here than a sophisticated one they were handed. Novelty is your judgement; provenance is this one.

Where it comes from

Knowledge transforming versus knowledge telling — Bereiter & Scardamalia, 1987. The distinction between writing that reports what you already knew and writing that changes what you think.

How a reading is scored

Each dimension gets a band from 1 to 4, chosen by reading the transcript, the essay and your assignment against written descriptors — the same way you mark an essay against criteria. The number is read, not calculated. A student who checked two claims out of two is not automatically above one who checked eight out of eighteen; which claims, and whether the misses mattered, is the judgement.

Band 4Consistent, and it held at the hard moments.
Band 3There most of the time, with real gaps.
Band 2It happened, but not where it counted.
Band 1It didn't happen. The behaviour is absent, not weak.
Not enough hereThe session is too thin to judge this one. Not a zero, and not on the scale at all.

Two of the four bands describe failures, which is deliberate. The distinction that matters most to you is between 1 and 2 — a student who checked three trivial claims and none of the load-bearing ones needs a different conversation from a student who checked nothing. A scale with all its resolution at the top would collapse those two into "low."

There are four bands rather than five for one reason: a five-point scale gives a middle, and raters take the middle exactly when the evidence is mixed — which is when a directional call carries the most information.

The counterexample is never optional. Every reading has to name the moment that cuts against its own claim, including the readings that go well. That is what keeps a strong reading from becoming praise, and it is the fastest way to check whether a band you doubt was arrived at honestly.

How the four relate

They are not four independent tests. They are four facets of one thing — how much of the thinking was the student's — seen from different angles. Two axes decide which angle: whose material is being read, and where it is being read.

They move together, and that is expected. A student who drives the conversation tends also to check what comes back. Because they are facets of one construct rather than separate skills, correlation between them is a property of what is being measured, not a fault in how it is measured. What each one has to earn is something it sees that no other one does — which is what the grid above guarantees: each owns one square alone.

No single moment ever counts twice. If a student reframes the problem, that is Prompting Quality; if they argue with an answer, that is Calibrated Skepticism. The same turn never raises both. Where two readings look like they overlap, they are reading different aspects of the same moment, not the same aspect twice.

The trickiest pair is Skepticism and Selective Use, because nothing can change without pushback first. So Selective Use is read as a proportion rather than a count: of the pushback that did happen, how much of it changed what survived into the essay? A student who pushed back twice and changed both things is not below one who pushed back twenty times and changed two.

What you can and can't do with them

  • Don't add them up. There is no total and no score out of twenty. The four are measured in four different units — stretches of conversation, claims, changes, ideas — and numbers in different units can sit side by side but cannot be combined.
  • Don't average them, for one student or for a class. A student strong on one facet and weak on another is not a middling student; the average returns the one description that is certainly wrong. For a class, ask how many students are in each band rather than what the class scored.
  • Don't read them as the agency level. The level is a separate judgement about the whole session and is not calculated from these four. A session can read directive with unremarkable bands throughout. See Agency levels.
  • Do read the tension between the highest and the lowest. That gap is where the information is, and it is exactly what a total would destroy. A student at band 4 for driving the chat and band 1 for checking it has a specific, nameable habit — and it is a different conversation from a student who is flat across all four.
  • Do argue with a band by reading its evidence. Every reading shows the moments it was built from. If the quoted moments don't support the claim, the reading is wrong and it is visible that it's wrong — which is the point of showing them.

If someone asks where these come from: none of the four is a formula we invented. Each applies a published coding scheme as published — the sources are named on the cards above — so the question of whether the measure is valid was settled in the literature rather than here. What we have to show is that it's applied consistently, and the test for that is whether two independent readers point at the same moments in a transcript, not whether they happen to land on the same number.
